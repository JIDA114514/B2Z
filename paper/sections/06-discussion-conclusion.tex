\section{Discussion, Limitations, and Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Our results separate single-transmitter encapsulation choices into protocol thresholds and measured wireless regimes. Structurally, \ble{} extensions $b\le 1$ fit directly into one legacy advertisement, whereas $b\ge 2$ trigger multi-frame \fragproto{} fragmentation (including the standalone \stdfour{} burst, single-attempt \modelc{} proxies at Point~A $(b,z){=}(0,0)$ and Point~B1 $(1,0)$ are \SI{1456}{\micro\second} and \SI{1464}{\micro\second}). In \texttt{R1} wireless measurements at Point~A, \modelc{} minimizes pooled unit cost at $K{=}1$ (\SI{2141.2}{\micro\second}/task), whereas under the tested \SI{6000}{\micro\second} budget cap all three modes approach saturation in within-planned-attempt joint resolution and the unit-cost ranking flips in favor of \modejc{} and \modesc{} (wireless unit cost was not measured at Point~B1). At five-fragment Point~B $(26,0)$, lower joint resolution is observed for ch39 \modelc{} across all three blocks while extended advertising avoids same-attempt multi-fragment exposure. Between co-channel \modejc{} and \modesc{}, the three blocks show no stable joint-resolution advantage, so \modejc{}'s concrete benefit is cutting bursts per attempt from $3$ to $2$ at equal single-attempt waveform budget alongside the deterministic $\num{400}+8b\,\si{\micro\second}$ offset.

Four validity boundaries limit generalization. First, \modejc{} (ch3, \SI{2410}{MHz}) vs.\ \modelc{} (ch39, \SI{2480}{MHz}) couples format with carrier frequency, and $n{=}3$ blocks ($\text{df}{=}2$) yield wide $t$-intervals crossing zero even at $+50.67$ and $+66.00$\,pp mean gaps. Additional blocks could improve estimation precision, whereas isolating the format effect would require controlling or independently varying the \ble{} channel. Second, cost metrics track submitted baseband waveform duration and polled DMA completion rather than RF power-meter energy or switching overhead, and uncalibrated HackRF clocks leave before-deadline RF delivery unaudited (\num{2730}/\num{3600} \texttt{unknown}). Third, \stdfour{} offset compensation depends on pre-configured $b$ rather than cold-start discovery or intra-run length changes. Fourth, all data come from a fixed-secondary SDR testbed; commercial \ble{}/ZigBee stack interoperability, online timing across dynamic \texttt{AuxPtr} channel hopping, and physical actuator synchronization remain unverified.

In summary, \proto{} co-packages independent \ble{} and BlueBee-emulated \stdfour{} control records with shared timing metadata inside one \ble{}~5 \texttt{AUX\_ADV\_IND} PDU, verified at $26/26$, $52/26$, and $28/32$\,B (\ble{}/\stdfour{}) complete record pairs (\SI{179}{B} and \SI{205}{B} payloads) with shared-IQ offsets matching $\num{400}+8b\,\si{\micro\second}$ and fixed-secondary feeder-disconnect recovery. Across 36 confirmatory runs (\num{3600} planned tasks), co-channel \modejc{} reduces bursts per attempt from $3$ to $2$ at equal single-attempt waveform budget relative to \modesc{} without a stable joint-resolution advantage, while relative to ch39 \modelc{} its unit-cost ranking flips with retry policy at Point~A and higher joint resolution is observed across all three blocks at five-fragment Point~B. Future work will add hardware-triggered shared clock instrumentation and commercial \ble{}~5/\stdfour{} receivers to audit strict RF deadlines and evaluate online timing continuity across dynamic \texttt{AuxPtr} following.
