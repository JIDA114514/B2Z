\section{Discussion, Limitations, and Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Four validity boundaries govern interpretation. First, ch3 \modejc{} (\SI{2410}{MHz}) vs.\ ch39 \modelc{} (\SI{2480}{MHz}) couples format with \ble{} frequency, and $n{=}3$ blocks ($\text{df}{=}2$) yield zero-crossing $t$-CIs even at $+50.67$ and $+66.00$\,pp mean gaps; additional blocks could improve precision, whereas isolating format effects requires controlling or independently varying the \ble{} channel (wireless unit cost was also not measured at Point~B1). Second, cost metrics track submitted baseband waveform duration and DMA submission rather than RF power-meter energy or switching overhead, and uncalibrated receiver clocks leave before-deadline RF delivery unaudited (\num{2730}/\num{3600} \texttt{unknown}). Third, \stdfour{} offset compensation relies on pre-shared $b$ rather than cold-start discovery. Fourth, our fixed-secondary SDR testbed does not verify commercial \ble{}/ZigBee stack interoperability, dynamic \texttt{AuxPtr} online timing, or actuator synchronization.

Within these boundaries, co-packaging independent \ble{} and BlueBee-emulated \stdfour{} records in one \ble{}~5 \texttt{AUX\_ADV\_IND} PDU ($26/26$, $52/26$, and $28/32$\,B verified with $400{+}8b\,\si{\micro\second}$ shared-IQ offsets and feeder-disconnect recovery) cuts co-channel bursts per attempt from $3$ to $2$ at equal single-attempt waveform duration without a consistent joint-resolution gain over \modesc{}. Relative to ch39 \modelc{}, short-record unit cost flips from legacy at $K{=}1$ to extended advertising under a \SI{6}{ms} cap as open-loop retries saturate, while avoiding five-fragment reassembly at \SI{52}{B} yields higher observed joint resolution across all three blocks in the tested channel configurations. Future work will add shared-clock RF instrumentation and commercial \ble{}~5/\stdfour{} receivers to audit strict deadline compliance, control \ble{} carrier channels directly, and test online timing across dynamic \texttt{AuxPtr} following.
