\section{Discussion and Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

\emph{Conclusions and design implications:} Co-packaging \ble{} and \stdfour{} commands inside one \ble{}~5 \texttt{AUX\_ADV\_IND} PDU yields three conclusions for single-transmitter systems. First, \proto{} integrates complete $26/26$, $52/26$, and $28/32$\,B pairs with $400{+}8b\,\si{\micro\second}$ offsets, cutting co-channel bursts from $3$ to $2$ per attempt at equal waveform duration (\modejc{} vs.\ \modesc{}) without losing joint resolution, while sustaining local slot timing and unilateral disconnect recovery. Second, for short records ($26/26$\,B), legacy \modelc{} is cheapest at $K{=}1$, whereas a \SI{6}{ms} cap saturates \modejc{}/\modesc{} at $K{=}3$ while forcing a fourth \modelc{} attempt, reversing unit cost ($-14.8\%$ \modejc{}, $-16.2\%$ \modesc{}) and showing retry granularity can outweigh single-shot compactness. Third, beyond the \SI{27}{B} ceiling ($52/26$\,B), single-PDU co-packaging avoids five-fragment \fragproto{} fragility, raising joint resolution in all three blocks ($238/300$ vs.\ $86/300$ at $K{=}1$; $291/300$ vs.\ $93/300$ at \SI{6}{ms}) and cutting unit cost by $69.4\text{--}76.9\%$ over ch39 \modelc{}.

\emph{Validity boundaries and future work:} Four boundaries govern extrapolation: (i) ch3 \modejc{} (\SI{2410}{MHz}) vs.\ ch39 \modelc{} (\SI{2480}{MHz}) couples format with carrier channel, $n{=}3$ blocks ($\text{df}{=}2$) yield zero-crossing $t$-CIs, and B1 omits wireless unit cost; (ii) cost tracks DMA baseband duration rather than RF energy or switching overhead, while uncalibrated clocks leave before-deadline compliance unverifiable across \num{2730}/\num{3600} jointly resolved tasks (\num{870}/\num{3600} not jointly decoded); (iii) \stdfour{} compensation uses pre-shared $b$; and (iv) the fixed-secondary SDR testbed omits commercial interoperability, dynamic \texttt{AuxPtr} timing, and actuator synchronization. Future work will add shared-clock instrumentation, channel-matched controls, commercial receivers, and dynamic \texttt{AuxPtr} following to audit strict deadlines.
