\section{Discussion and Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

\emph{Conclusions and design implications:} Co-packaging \ble{} and \stdfour{} in one \texttt{AUX\_ADV\_IND} PDU yields three findings. First, \proto{} carries $26/26$, $52/26$, and $28/32$\,B pairs with $400{+}8b\,\si{\micro\second}$ offsets, sustains local slot timing and unilateral recovery, and cuts total bursts per attempt from $3$ to $2$ at equal waveform duration (\modejc{} vs.\ \modesc{}), while neither mode showed a consistent joint-resolution advantage across the four conditions. Second, for short records ($26/26$\,B), \modelc{} achieved the lowest pooled unit cost at $K{=}1$; under the \SI{6}{ms} open-loop policy, \modejc{} and \modesc{} used three attempts vs.\ four for \modelc{}, yielding $14.8\%$ and $16.2\%$ lower pooled unit costs and showing that retry granularity shifts budget-capped rankings. Third, at $52/26$\,B, \modejc{} carried the \ble{} record in one secondary PDU while \modelc{} required five fragments; ch3 \modejc{} resolved $291/300$ vs.\ $93/300$ for ch39 \modelc{} under \SI{6}{ms} (pooled unit cost $-76.9\%$ at $K{=}1$, $-69.4\%$ at \SI{6}{ms}), without isolating fragmentation.

\emph{Validity boundaries and future work:} Four boundaries govern extrapolation: (i) ch3 \modejc{} (\SI{2410}{MHz}) vs.\ ch39 \modelc{} (\SI{2480}{MHz}) couples format and channel, $n{=}3$ blocks ($\text{df}{=}2$) yield zero-crossing $t$-CIs, and B1 omits wireless unit cost; (ii) cost is the total duration of submitted baseband waveforms rather than RF energy or switching, while uncalibrated clocks leave before-deadline compliance unverifiable across \num{2730}/\num{3600} jointly resolved tasks (\num{870}/\num{3600} not jointly decoded); (iii) \stdfour{} compensation uses pre-shared $b$; and (iv) the fixed-secondary SDR testbed omits commercial interoperability, dynamic \texttt{AuxPtr} timing, and actuator synchronization. Future work will add shared-clock instrumentation, channel-matched controls, and commercial receivers with dynamic \texttt{AuxPtr} following to audit strict deadlines.
